## PART 2 — THE PART I ACTUALLY WORK ON

**1. SBS's "Digital Branch" shows what tablet-based branch tooling actually saves**
SBS launched a cloud-native, tablet-based branch servicing platform for UK building societies and banks that replaces legacy teller terminals, letting staff roam the branch with a single customer view; it's measured at ~1,946 staff hours saved per branch/year and up to 20% lower teller IT costs. — [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/109920/sbs-bids-to-replace-teller-systems-with-tablet-based-branch-banking-platform)
*Operational read:* This is a concrete before/after for exactly our branch tooling question — mobile, single-view servicing vs. terminal-tied teller systems — with hard numbers attached, not just a vendor claim.
*One action:* Ask Branch Ops/IT what share of current teller downtime and infra cost traces to on-prem terminal hardware, and whether that's ever been sized as a mobile/tablet business case.

**2. OJK cut SLIK credit-record update time from 1–1.5 months to 3 working days**
Effective July 1, lenders must update a debtor's SLIK status (e.g., loan paid off) within three working days, down from as much as six weeks; OJK also now excludes financing under Rp1M from mandatory reporting to keep the dataset relevant. — [Fintech News Indonesia](https://fintechnews.id/110254/lending/ojk-slik-credit-data-updates-indonesia/), [VOI](https://voi.id/en/economy/583452)
*Operational read:* Stale SLIK data has been a real source of SME scoring friction — an applicant who paid off a loan still reads as indebted for weeks. A 3-day mandate should shrink that lag across the market, changing what "normal" credit-data freshness looks like for our own scoring checks.
*One action:* Check how our SME scoring flow handles SLIK data age today, and whether we're positioned to benefit once market-wide update speed improves — or whether our own reporting into SLIK meets the new 3-day bar.

**3. Industry-wide, one in five onboarding applications still dies on KYC friction**
Benchmarks show ~20% of digital KYC checks fail outright, 70% of users abandon flows that run past three minutes, and a customer asked to re-upload a document is 3x more likely to bail — costing the industry an estimated $3.3B/year in lost onboarding. — [Fenergo](https://resources.fenergo.com/newsroom/share-of-banks-losing-clients-to-poor-kyc-practices-surges-to-record-high)
*Operational read:* The re-upload stat is the actionable one — it points straight at document-capture quality (lighting, glare, crop) as a disproportionate abandonment driver, more than the verification logic itself.
*One action:* Pull our own onboarding funnel and check re-upload rate and time-to-complete specifically — if re-uploads are common, the fix is capture UX, not KYC policy.
